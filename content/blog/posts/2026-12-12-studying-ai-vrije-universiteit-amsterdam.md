In September 2026 I started the **BSc Artificial Intelligence at Vrije Universiteit Amsterdam**. On paper it's an unusual move: I already work as a Technical Product Owner, I've been building websites since 2022, and I've owned products used across an entire industry. Why go back to the lecture hall?

Here's my reasoning — and what the first months have been like.

## Product experience shows you where the ceiling is

Owning products teaches you to see what's possible with the tools you have. It also shows you exactly where those tools stop. Again and again I've sketched features that needed real intelligence — predicting which users are about to leave, personalising journeys automatically, turning messy data into decisions — and been limited by how deeply I understood the models behind them.

I didn't want to keep calling AI a black box. I wanted to understand it from the foundations up.

## Why a full degree, not a course

Online courses are great for learning a tool. A degree builds the foundations underneath the tools: mathematics, programming, logic, and machine learning theory. Those foundations don't go out of date when the next framework arrives, and they're what let you judge whether an AI feature is realistic before you promise it on a roadmap.

## Why Vrije Universiteit Amsterdam

A few reasons made the decision easy:

- **A broad AI programme** that builds solid foundations rather than chasing a single tool or trend.
- **Amsterdam** — an international city with a serious tech scene, where studying and working can happen side by side.
- **English-language teaching**, with the Netherlands' famously practical approach to everything.

## Studying and working at the same time

I'm studying alongside my role as Technical Product Owner for CRM & MarTech at Voveron. It's demanding, but the two feed each other constantly. A lecture on classification makes me rethink how we segment users; a real product problem gives abstract theory a concrete shape. Having already balanced the IB Diploma with product work in Yerevan, I knew what I was signing up for.

## From Yerevan to Amsterdam

Moving countries is a bigger change than any job title. The practical parts — housing, paperwork, bikes — sort themselves out. The rewarding part is the mix of people: classmates from all over the world, each bringing a different way of thinking about problems.

I'm learning **Dutch** too — currently around A2, and climbing every week.

## What I want to build next

My goal hasn't changed since I wrote my first line of code: build things people actually use. The difference is the depth I want to build them with. Products that learn from behaviour, CRM that adapts to each user, tools that turn data into decisions automatically — that's the intersection of product and AI where I want to spend my career.
