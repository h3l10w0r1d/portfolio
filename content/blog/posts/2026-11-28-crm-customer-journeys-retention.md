iGaming is one of the most demanding environments for CRM. Users have endless alternatives a tap away, regulations differ by market, and every message competes with a dozen others. If a customer journey works there, it'll work almost anywhere.

As a CRM Specialist at Playtronix I designed customer journeys aimed at engagement and retention, owned the bonus-granting architecture end to end, and ran a promotion calendar tuned for different nations and religions. Here's what that taught me.

## 1. Trigger on behaviour, not on the calendar

The weakest CRM is a weekly newsletter sent to everyone. The strongest reacts to what a user just did — or stopped doing:

- **Onboarding** right after sign-up, guiding toward the first meaningful action.
- **First-value moments** — celebrating or nudging the first success.
- **Drop-off signals** — a user who was active daily and suddenly isn't.
- **Win-back** — a different message for someone gone a week versus three months.

Each journey should have one clear goal and an exit condition. When a user completes the goal, stop messaging them about it.

## 2. Segment by intent, not just demographics

Age and country are easy segments, but intent predicts behaviour better: new versus returning, high versus low engagement, price-sensitive versus not. Start with a handful of segments you can actually act on rather than fifty you can't.

## 3. Treat incentives as a product, not a promotion

Bonuses, discounts and rewards are powerful — and dangerous. Owning the bonus-granting architecture taught me to treat it like a product with its own rules:

- **Clear eligibility** — who gets what, and why.
- **Guardrails** — limits, expiry and protection against abuse.
- **Auditability** — every grant traceable to a rule.
- **Measurement** — did the incentive change behaviour, or just reward what would have happened anyway?

An incentive system without guardrails isn't generosity; it's a leak.

## 4. Localise the calendar, not just the language

A promotion calendar for multiple countries can't be one calendar translated. Holidays, weekends and important dates differ by nation and religion, and getting them wrong ranges from awkward to offensive. Build the calendar per market, with the people who know those markets, and plan it well ahead.

## 5. Respect the inbox

Frequency caps, quiet hours, channel preferences and an easy unsubscribe aren't just compliance — they protect the channel itself. Every irrelevant message lowers the chance the next one gets read.

## 6. Measure retention in cohorts

Open and click rates tell you whether a message was interesting. **Cohort retention** tells you whether it mattered. Compare users who entered a journey with a holdout group that didn't, and judge journeys on the difference.

## 7. Choose tools last

I've worked with platforms like Customer.io, Brevo, Solitics and Xtremepush. They're all capable. The difference between a great and a mediocre CRM programme is rarely the tool — it's clean event data, clear goals and disciplined testing.
