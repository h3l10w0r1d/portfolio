People sometimes ask how a freelance web developer from Yerevan ended up as a Technical Product Owner. The honest answer: one step at a time, each one teaching something the previous step couldn't. Here's the path, and what I took from each stage.

## 2021: learning to program

It started with a certified course in **Programming with Python** in 2021 — foundations of programming and computational thinking. Nothing glamorous, but it gave me the habit that matters most: breaking a big problem into small ones you can actually solve.

## 2022: freelance web developer

In March 2022 I started freelancing as a web developer. Since then I've worked with **50+ clients** across industries and countries — landing pages, business websites, booking systems, membership platforms and full web-app architectures.

What freelancing taught me:

- **Scoping is a skill.** Clients rarely know exactly what they need. Turning "we want a website" into a concrete list of pages and features is half the job.
- **You own the whole lifecycle.** Design, build, SEO, delivery, and the phone call when something breaks. There's nobody to hand off to.
- **Shipping beats perfect.** A site that's live and earning is worth more than a perfect one that isn't.

## 2023: CRM Specialist

In July 2023 I joined Playtronix as a **CRM Specialist**. This was my first time working on a product with a large user base, and it changed how I think about software.

I designed customer journeys that lifted engagement and retention, owned the bonus-granting architecture end to end, and ran a promotion calendar tuned for different nations and religions. The lesson: **software is only as good as the behaviour it changes.** I stopped thinking in features and started thinking in user outcomes.

## 2024: Product Owner

A year later, in July 2024, I became **Product Owner** — leading the vision and strategic direction for several products in the iGaming industry: a CMS, an affiliate-management platform, a CRM system and a streaming service. Along the way I founded and built **Adrenaline**, a business-intelligence platform for partners.

At the same time I was finishing the **IB Diploma at Quantum College** in Yerevan, which taught me more about time management than any productivity book.

What product ownership taught me:

- **Saying no is the job.** A roadmap is a list of things you've decided *not* to do yet.
- **Translation is everything.** Engineers, designers and stakeholders speak different languages. The PO's job is to make sure everyone is building the same thing.
- **A developer's background is an unfair advantage.** I can read the stack trace, estimate honestly, and spot when "simple" isn't.

## 2026: Technical Product Owner and AI student

In July 2026 I joined **Voveron as Technical Product Owner for CRM & MarTech**, and in September I started the **BSc Artificial Intelligence at Vrije Universiteit Amsterdam**. Moving from Yerevan to Amsterdam was the biggest single change on this list — and the one I'm most excited about.

## What I'd tell a developer thinking about product

1. **Keep building.** Your technical credibility is your edge — don't let it rust.
2. **Get close to users.** Take a role, even a small one, where you see real behaviour data.
3. **Learn to write.** Clear user stories, clear decisions, clear "no"s.
4. **Own something end to end.** Nothing teaches product thinking faster.
