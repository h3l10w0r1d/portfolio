"Technical Product Owner, CRM & MarTech" is a mouthful of a job title. Friends ask what it means; recruiters ask how it differs from a regular Product Owner. Here's how I'd explain it — from the role I hold today at Voveron and the product roles before it.

## Product Owner, with the "technical" part doing real work

A Product Owner owns *what* gets built and *why*: the vision, the backlog, the priorities, and the acceptance of finished work. The "technical" part means you're expected to go deeper into *how* — reading API docs, understanding data models, discussing architecture trade-offs with engineers, and writing user stories precise enough to build without a dozen follow-up meetings.

It doesn't mean you write production code every day. It means engineers don't have to translate for you.

## What "CRM & MarTech" adds

CRM and marketing technology sit between three groups that rarely speak the same language:

- **Marketing and CRM teams**, who want to launch campaigns and journeys quickly.
- **Engineering**, who build and maintain the systems underneath.
- **Data and analytics**, who need clean, trustworthy events to measure anything.

The MarTech product owner's job is to make those three work as one system. In practice that covers areas like customer data and event tracking, segmentation, messaging channels (email, push, SMS, in-app), journey and campaign tooling, incentive systems, and the integrations that move data between them.

## A typical week

No two weeks are identical, but most include:

- **Backlog refinement** — turning requests from marketing ("we need a win-back flow for lapsed users") into stories with clear data requirements and acceptance criteria.
- **Prioritisation** — weighing a shiny new channel against fixing the event that half the segments depend on. (The event usually wins.)
- **Integration work** — mapping fields between systems, agreeing on definitions, and checking edge cases before they become incidents.
- **Stakeholder conversations** — explaining trade-offs in plain language and saying "not yet" kindly.
- **Reviewing results** — did last sprint's change actually move a metric?

## Skills that matter most

1. **Data literacy.** If you can't reason about events, identities and attribution, you can't own a CRM product.
2. **Systems thinking.** Every MarTech change touches something else. Draw the diagram before you write the story.
3. **Writing.** Clear stories and clear decision records save more time than any tool.
4. **Privacy awareness.** Consent, preferences and data protection rules aren't someone else's problem — they shape what you can build, especially in the EU.
5. **Empathy for operators.** The best MarTech product lets the CRM team move fast without filing a ticket for every campaign.
